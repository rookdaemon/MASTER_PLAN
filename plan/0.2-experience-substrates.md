# 0.2 Subjective Experience Substrates [ARCHITECT]
## Description

Tier 2 of the Master Plan. Create durable physical systems capable of sustaining conscious processes, and enable migration of biological minds to new substrates.

Covers two goals:
- **S1 — Enduring Substrates Constructed:** Fault-tolerant, self-repairing, energy-independent, long-term stable systems (radiation-hardened computation, self-repairing nanofabrication, long-duration energy, consciousness-preserving redundancy).
- **S2 — Subjective Experience Migration:** Transfer or instantiation of biological minds within new substrates (brain emulation, continuity-preserving transfer, identity persistence verification, hybrid cognition).

For this card, **energy independence** means autonomous acquisition, conversion, storage, and transition among declared ambient or carried energy sources without ongoing biological-civilization support. It does not mean operation without energy input. Depletion of every declared source is outside the qualified operating envelope and must be reported explicitly rather than treated as an untested claim of indefinite operation.

## Architecture Integration

S1 supplies S2 with a qualified target substrate whose failure recovery preserves the computational state and temporal continuity required by a conscious process. S2 supplies the emulation, transfer, and identity-verification protocols used to validate that preservation claim. A substrate is therefore not Tier-2-qualified from durability testing alone: the same integrated system must pass both the S1 resilience tests and the S2 continuity and identity tests.

The integration sequence is:
1. Qualify the substrate's computation, repair, energy, and redundancy properties under S1.
2. Obtain documented informed consent and independent ethical and scientific authorization, then establish pre-transfer identity, continuity, and welfare baselines under S2.
3. Migrate or instantiate the mind on the qualified substrate.
4. Repeat identity and continuity verification during nominal operation and injected S1 failure-and-repair events.

### Implementation Architecture

The repository integration layer coordinates, but does not reimplement, the S1 and S2 child systems. A qualification coordinator consumes injectable S1 evidence, S2 migration and identity observations, independent-authorization records, and evidence storage through explicit ports. Each qualification call receives its observation timestamp from the caller; the coordinator must not read a system clock or environment-specific resource directly.

The coordinator emits an immutable record containing the substrate-configuration ID, migration-protocol ID, threshold-registry version, observation windows, every predeclared failure-matrix result, authorization references, deviations, and a pass/fail verdict. Tests are written first with in-memory ports and must prove that qualification is refused for missing or stale authorization, mismatched substrate configurations, incomplete scenarios, threshold or welfare violations, failed abort or recovery actions, and missing child evidence. Passing repository tests demonstrates orchestration and evidence validation only; physical S1 performance and live-subject S2 outcomes require independently attributable external evidence and may not be inferred from simulation or mocked results.

Temporal validity is request data, not ambient state. Each qualification request supplies `evaluatedAt`; each authorization record supplies immutable `validFrom` and `validUntil` bounds; and each child observation supplies `observedAt` plus its declared observation-window bounds. The coordinator rejects authorization unless `validFrom <= evaluatedAt <= validUntil`, rejects observations outside their declared windows, and rejects any record timestamp later than `evaluatedAt`. All timestamps use unambiguous ISO-8601 instants with offsets, and tests pass them explicitly rather than reading a clock.

## Acceptance Criteria
- S1: A physical substrate satisfies the fault-tolerance, autonomous-repair, energy-independence, and consciousness-preserving redundancy criteria defined by `0.2.1` and its children.
- S2: At least one biological mind satisfies the emulation, continuity, identity-persistence, and hybrid-cognition criteria defined by `0.2.2` and its children on an S1-qualified substrate.
- Test contract: Before migration, the S1 and S2 protocols jointly declare numerical continuity and identity thresholds, observation windows, and a failure matrix covering loss and recovery of every component class whose failure could interrupt conscious processing, at least one autonomous repair, and every supported energy-source transition.
- Integration: Every declared failure-matrix scenario is executed on the same substrate configuration used for migration; the migrated mind remains within the predeclared thresholds throughout each observation window, with no unplanned interruption of conscious processing.
- Ethical authorization: Before any live-subject migration or instantiation, an independent body with no execution conflict documents approval of the protocol's scientific validity, consent-capacity assessment, welfare limits, abort and recovery paths, and treatment of every resulting conscious instance. No irreversible subject procedure may begin until its declared reversible validation stages pass, and no resulting instance may be terminated, erased, or abandoned merely because qualification fails.
- Subject safety: Before testing, the protocol records informed consent, predeclared welfare limits, abort triggers, and a tested recovery or safe-suspension path; exceeding a welfare limit or failing an abort or recovery action fails qualification.
- Implementation contract: The test-first qualification coordinator uses only injected child, authorization, and evidence ports; accepts `evaluatedAt` and all record timestamps as explicit request data; enforces the authorization-validity, observation-window, and no-future-record rules defined above; emits the complete immutable qualification record; and refuses every invalid or incomplete case enumerated in the implementation architecture.
- Evidence boundary: Repository simulations, mocks, and unit or integration tests cannot satisfy the physical-substrate or live-subject criteria; every such claim cites independently attributable evidence identifying the tested configuration, protocol, subject authorization, observation window, and result.
- Traceability: Final synthesis records the qualifying evidence from both children, the substrate configuration tested, the migration protocol used, and the results of the integrated failure-and-repair tests.
