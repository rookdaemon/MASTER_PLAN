# Space and Longevity

**Status:** Speculative

## Scope

This dossier consolidates planet-independent habitats, asteroid resources, autonomous space
manufacturing, replication, propulsion, probe swarms, colony seeding, distributed consciousness,
stellar engineering, compact-object energy, cosmological horizons, and far-future thermodynamics.
These areas remain visible without receiving near-term eligibility merely because they are
important at long timescales.

## Planet-independent infrastructure

A viable site of conscious life must be more than a surviving computer or habitat shell. Proposed
qualification covers:

- power generation, storage, distribution, and black-start;
- conduction and radiation heat rejection in vacuum;
- radiation, charging, micrometeoroids, dust, and thermal cycling;
- atmosphere, water, nutrients, materials, spares, and waste where biological life is involved;
- communication delay, navigation, timing, and local control;
- maintenance, manufacturing, metrology, and failure-domain separation;
- welfare, agency, social institutions, and legitimate authority; and
- independent viability under prolonged loss of Earth support.

Repository power budgets, thermal models, radiation specifications, platform tiers, and
communication protocols are proposals. They do not constitute a qualified spacecraft or habitat.

## Resource utilization and manufacturing

Asteroid and in-space resource work models prospecting, extraction, processing, storage, transport,
and depot operations. A real program would need independently measured composition, energy and
delta-v budgets, equipment reliability, contamination and debris controls, ownership and legal
analysis, environmental impact, and failure recovery.

Autonomous manufacturing adds design provenance, metrology, calibration, feedstock closure, tool
replacement, software integrity, bounded operating authority, and safe shutdown. Material access
does not authorize self-expansion.

## Propulsion and interstellar probes

Candidate propulsion concepts should be compared through mass, energy, thrust, specific impulse,
efficiency, thermal limits, reliability, launch and braking, communication, navigation, collision
risk, and achievable test evidence. No concept is selected merely for theoretical performance.

Probe architectures require long-duration fault tolerance, bounded autonomy, repair, navigation,
scientific integrity, non-contamination, lawful operation, and governance under communication
delays. A von Neumann architecture remains a design pattern, not permission to replicate.

## Self-replication

Replication introduces exceptional tail risk and is gated behind verified planet-independent
capability, welfare precautions, cross-substrate coexistence governance, resource accounting, and
dependencies. Proposed protocols require:

- explicit blueprints and provenance;
- replication-depth and resource bounds;
- mutation and error detection;
- authentication, authorization, and revocation;
- independent population and environmental measurement;
- pause, shutdown, containment, and recovery;
- prevention of credential or governance replication by default; and
- a rights process for any probable moral patient affected or instantiated.

Software tests of generational stability do not demonstrate physical closure, safe autonomy, or
legitimate expansion.

## Colonies and distributed networks

Colony seeding raises viability, founder effects, consent, representation, cultural divergence,
knowledge preservation, ecological impact, and relations with unknown life. Distributed
consciousness additionally raises latency, partition, synchronization, identity, fork, merge, and
welfare questions. Network coherence cannot be equated with unified experience.

Independent viability means surviving and governing without hidden Earth dependencies while
preserving the ability to revise goals. It does not mean loyalty to a frozen doctrine.

## Stellar and cosmological horizons

Stellar engineering, compact-object energy extraction, horizon-aware expansion, reversible
computation, deep hibernation, and heat-death mitigation remain speculative research. Each proposal
must separate established physics, engineering extrapolation, and unknown physics.

Far-future value depends heavily on unresolved questions about consciousness, identity, energy,
cosmology, coordination, and discounting. These topics can improve long-range option awareness but
must not crowd out credible near-term preservation.

## Implemented artifacts

**Implemented:** The repository contains simulations and typed models for asteroid operations,
space missions, habitats, propulsion, replication, colonies, distributed networks, stellar
resources, and selected cosmological scenarios. Tests establish local software behavior under
declared parameters.

**Observed:** Model runs can reveal inconsistent budgets, missing interfaces, fault sensitivity, and
assumption dependence. They are not mission data, material tests, launch readiness, or proof that a
self-sustaining off-world society is feasible.

## Evidence and limitations

Spacecraft engineering, planetary science, astronomy, thermodynamics, and current mission data can
bound individual assumptions. The consolidated documents contain many unvalidated design choices
and toy thresholds. External qualification must use current primary sources, measured hardware,
independent review, and mission-specific uncertainty.

No repository artifact demonstrates a planet-independent conscious civilization, autonomous
industrial closure, safe self-replication, interstellar propulsion, colony viability, distributed
subjective unity, stellar engineering, or mitigation of cosmological limits.
