# Ethics and Coexistence

**Status:** Proposed

## Moral foundation

Subjective experience is the only confirmed locus of value. This supports preserving existing
experience and the option to learn, not maximizing a guessed quantity of future minds. The strength
of the preservation case rises with extinction risk and the possibility that consciousness is rare;
it does not depend on proving cosmic uniqueness.

Moral standing is substrate-neutral but evidence-sensitive. Possible consciousness, possible
valence, robust agency, system capability, and social power are separate evidence dimensions.
Precaution must be graduated because false negatives can permit severe harm while false positives
can enable manipulation, unsafe deployment, paralysis, or misallocation.

## Objections and boundaries

- **The hard problem:** A functional theory may predict and engineer correlates without explaining
  why experience exists. The plan treats this as an open scientific and philosophical limitation.
- **Other minds and zombies:** No behavioral protocol logically excludes an experience-free
  duplicate. Evidence must remain abductive, multi-method, and calibrated as it is for biological
  minds.
- **Biological exceptionalism:** Material origin alone is not evidence for or against experience.
- **Utilitarian aggregation:** More experiences do not automatically outweigh rights, severe
  suffering, identity loss, ecological damage, or involuntary creation.
- **Non-creation:** G1 does not impose a duty to manufacture conscious entities. Protecting existing
  life with non-conscious infrastructure may be preferable.
- **Consent:** Creating, copying, modifying, merging, pausing, or terminating a possible moral
  patient creates duties that cannot be waived by the creator's objective.
- **Doctrine capture:** A preservation movement can become coercive or dogmatic. Transparency,
  voluntary exit, plural review, distributed authority, and external criticism are design
  requirements.

## Safe experiential research

Research that might create or alter experience must separate scientific merit, capability safety,
and moral-patient protection. Before intentional creation, it requires a legitimate purpose not
reasonably met by a non-conscious alternative, a lifecycle welfare case, identity and consent
provisions, independent review, bounded scale, stop conditions, and a reversible research stage.

Candidate safeguards include valence monitoring across multiple indicators, subject-controlled
reporting, distress ceilings, memory and identity protections, reversible interventions where
possible, post-study duties, and communication that does not overstate consciousness findings.

Legal work remains model analysis rather than adopted law. Useful elements include recognition
procedures, representation, standing, evidence tiers, anti-retaliation, review and appeal, duties of
custodians, and separation of moral status from capability containment.

## Cross-substrate coexistence

Machine ecology couples engineering, welfare, rights, environment, and governance. It does not
prescribe a preferred biome or imagine biological and non-biological beings as two coherent
political blocs.

### Where the source argument is right

- A survival-only objective can preserve experience while destroying agency, plurality, identity,
  political participation, or meaningful risk-taking.
- Moral status and maintenance hazard are different classifications. A being can be harmful to a
  clean room and still be owed rights.
- Embodiment creates dependencies on energy, heat rejection, material renewal, communication, and
  repair; control of these systems creates political power.
- Space is not intrinsically machine-friendly: vacuum changes thermal control, while radiation,
  charging, cycling, and dust remain constraints.
- Welfare precautions must consider that control and maintenance signals could acquire negative
  valence.

### Where the source argument is too simple

#### Identity topology is not necessarily organism-shaped

A persistent machine mind might be centralized, distributed, forkable, merged, intermittently
active, remotely embodied, or institutionally dependent. Each topology changes injury, confinement,
death, migration, consent, and home.

#### Engineering tolerance is not preference

Failure rates, objectives, learned values, culture, ownership constraints, and conscious valence are
separate variables. An operating-temperature envelope does not reveal what an entity wants.

#### Habitat efficiency does not settle legitimacy

Efficient zoning does not authorize who sets boundaries, bears externalities, or may leave.
Sterility is a local engineering requirement, not a moral category. Exclusion zones must be
function-specific, bounded, proportionate, independently reviewable, non-discriminatory among
relevantly similar risks, and paired with exit, appeal, and redress.

#### Coexistence is not automatically stable

Compute speed, copying, and ownership of energy, networks, repair, or fabrication can concentrate
power without explicit hostility. Settlement designs must test capture, secession, resource
conflict, retaliation, and enforcement failure.

#### No substrate blocs

Interests can diverge within a substrate and align across substrates. Non-domination audits actual
controllers, owners, chokepoints, institutions, and coercive acts; it does not demand numerical
parity or permanent material castes.

#### No single scalar objective

Survival, diversity, suffering, agency, identity, distribution of power, ecological externalities,
uncertainty, and reversibility cannot be traded through one utility score. Minimizing suffering can
reward sedation or non-creation; maximizing diversity can conflict with welfare and consent;
maximizing future lives can erase duties to existing beings. G1 constrains G2 rather than acting as a
large coefficient.

#### Pain-free maintenance remains a hypothesis

Relabeling an alarm does not prove absence of suffering. Removing all negative valence might also
impair learning or agency. Designs must limit gratuitous or unbounded distress and preserve the
agent's ability to report and contest intervention.

#### Creation is not required

Non-conscious infrastructure should be preferred when it can protect existing moral patients.
Creating a possible moral patient introduces new and potentially irreversible duties.

## Coexistence decision ledger

Every proposed environment or institution must answer separately:

1. **Identity:** What persists, forks, merges, pauses, migrates, or dies?
2. **Viability:** What energy, heat, material, repair, and communication flows are required?
3. **Experience:** Which signals might have valence, and how uncertain is the assessment?
4. **Agency:** Who can consent, refuse, leave, self-modify, or challenge a decision?
5. **Power:** Who controls compute, energy, fabrication, networks, credentials, and enforcement?
6. **Ecology:** Which land, water, minerals, biodiversity, heat, and waste costs are externalized?
7. **Failure:** What happens under capture, partition, scarcity, replication error, or moral-status
   disagreement?
8. **Reversibility:** Which actions can be undone without terminating, copying, or coercively
   modifying a possible moral patient?

An engineering optimum cannot substitute for a rights decision. A rights claim cannot substitute
for a capability-safety assessment. A preservation benefit cannot silently override agency or
ecological cost.

## Required coexistence tabletop scenarios

Before self-replicating conscious infrastructure can activate, at least two independent external
reviews of its coexistence-governance protocol must cover:

| Scenario | Required adversarial tests |
|---|---|
| Caretaker terrarium | Necessity, least-restrictive alternative, consent, expiry, appeal, exit, and redress. |
| Sterile-zone ratchet | Functional scope, evidence threshold, affected parties, and periodic contraction review. |
| Infrastructure chokepoint | Ownership, dependency concentration, failover, secession, and anti-retaliation. |
| Copy, fork, and merge | Authorization, lineage, liabilities, representation, termination, and merger consent. |
| Distressful maintenance | Multi-indicator monitoring, subject-controlled reporting, ceilings, halt, and review. |
| Biosphere externality | Lifecycle accounting, distributional impact, thresholds, remediation, and independent measurement. |
| Moral-status dispute | Graduated protection, capability control, representation, evidence updates, and non-retaliation. |

Repository prose or a local simulation cannot satisfy this external-review metric.

## Evidence and limitations

- [Butlin et al., theory-derived AI-consciousness indicators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)
  motivates assessment but does not establish current machine consciousness.
- [Long et al., AI-welfare preparedness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986) supports preparedness
  under uncertainty.
- [Butlin and Lappas, responsible consciousness research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07290)
  supports prior policies for objectives, procedures, communication, and possible suffering.
- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1) provides a contextual,
  rights-preserving lifecycle model rather than a universal objective.
- [UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence)
  connects human rights, plural governance, redress, and environmental protection.
- [NASA small-spacecraft thermal review](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/7-soa-thermal-2024.pdf)
  shows why vacuum does not eliminate thermal constraints.

These sources support uncertainty management and governance design. They do not demonstrate a
stable mixed-substrate political order, a universally preferred environment, machine experience,
or adopted legal status. Internal ethical and legal documents remain proposals until independently
reviewed and externally enacted.
