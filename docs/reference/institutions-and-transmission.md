# Institutions and Transmission

**Status:** Proposed

## Scope

This dossier consolidates the project's doctrine, narrative, practice, identity, education,
advocacy, institutional design, funding, succession, cultural evolution, knowledge preservation,
and propagation reasoning. Its goal is durable critical commitment, not unquestioning adherence.

## Inner doctrine and outer credo

The inner doctrine exposes axioms, derivations, objections, uncertainty, and boundary conditions for
scrutiny. The outer credo is a memorable expression of the same moral concern. The outer layer may
translate across secular-scientific, existential, philosophical, or spiritual registers, but it may
not conceal disagreement or replace the inner argument.

Every public claim must provide a path to sources, limitations, and correction. Emotional force is
not evidence. Scientific uncertainty must remain visible even when it weakens recruitment.

## Narrative

The retained narrative is simple: awareness emerged in a largely indifferent universe; confirmed
experience is vulnerable; extinction and needless suffering matter; science, care, institutions,
and responsible engineering can improve the odds. The antagonist is entropy and silence, not a
person, species, substrate, nation, critic, or worldview.

Narrative testing should reject fear manipulation, inevitability claims, manufactured persecution,
hero worship, hidden doctrine, and promises of immortality. Alternate framings should be evaluated
for accuracy, accessibility, exclusion, and downstream behavior.

## Practice

Proposed practices make consciousness and interdependence vivid through reflection, dialogue,
commemoration, study, service, scientific replication, and stewardship. Participation is voluntary;
practices must disclose purpose, avoid induced dependency, and provide easy exit.

No ritual establishes a metaphysical claim. Practice is evaluated through wellbeing, understanding,
retention, plural participation, correction, and adverse-event reporting rather than obedience or
conversion alone.

## Identity and belonging

Graduated participation can include reader, participant, practitioner, contributor, and steward,
without creating moral rank. Safeguards include:

- voluntary entry and exit without retaliation;
- no isolation from outside relationships or criticism;
- transparent finances, governance, doctrine, and conflicts;
- distributed and time-bounded authority;
- independent complaints, appeal, and redress;
- no out-group enemy or substrate caste;
- privacy and non-discrimination; and
- periodic review for coercive dynamics.

Belonging serves mutual support and sustained work. It must not become evidence of truth or a source
of special standing over non-members.

## Institutional architecture

Useful institutional functions include research coordination, education, evidence review, ethical
oversight, lawful advocacy, archives, funding, incident response, and constitutional stewardship.
No single institution should control doctrine, evidence, funding, credentials, and enforcement.

Capture resistance uses plural governance, conflict-of-interest disclosure, independent audit,
transparent budgets, succession exercises, fork and schism procedures, jurisdictional diversity,
and protected dissent. Institutions must be able to retire failed programs and amend strategy
without erasing historical assessments.

## Knowledge preservation

Durable knowledge requires more than copies. It needs provenance, interpretable formats, integrity
checks, independent custodians, migration, restoration exercises, context, translation, and a living
community able to criticize and update it.

The project retains content-addressing and geographically diverse storage as useful ideas, but
automatic publication is not treated as an operating prerequisite. Repository distribution must
respect credentials, privacy, source licenses, security boundaries, and the authority to publish.

## Funding and succession

Funding concentration can silently redirect priorities. The institutional dependency model tracks
source concentration, restricted funds, runway, conflicts, reporting duties, and the ability to stop
harmful work. Succession planning tests loss of founders, maintainers, reviewers, credentials,
funders, hosting, and legal entities.

A stored succession document is not readiness. Exercises require independent participants, measured
recovery, updated access, decision authority, and recorded failures.

## Growth and adoption

Awareness, understanding, participation, contribution, and stewardship form a possible adoption
path, not a sales funnel that licenses manipulation. Evaluation should track comprehension,
retention, contribution quality, diversity, dropout, complaints, corrections, and adverse effects.

Network effects can spread error as easily as insight. Opposition may reveal real defects; the
project must not manufacture persecution, become insular, or interpret criticism as proof of
importance.

## Consolidated finding

**Observed:** The institutional dependency analysis identified capture, schism, succession,
funding, provenance, and external-evidence gaps. It separated existing repository artifacts from
organizational readiness and real-world continuity. The work is a source-limited map, not evidence
that durable institutions exist.

**Implemented:** Repository governance encodes bounded authority, independent review for protected
changes, deterministic checks, exceptional escalation, and auditable strategy state. These controls
apply to the repository; they do not establish broader institutional legitimacy.

## Evidence and limitations

Institutional design can draw on nonprofit governance, research integrity, safety culture,
constitutional design, archival science, social-movement research, and high-reliability
organizations. The internal proposals have not been validated through a multi-generation
institution, broad community, independent funding ecosystem, or real succession crisis.

Metrics for adoption and culture are vulnerable to gaming and Goodhart effects. External review,
participant protection, lawful operation, transparent failures, and willingness to dissolve harmful
structures are required before claiming institutional continuity.
