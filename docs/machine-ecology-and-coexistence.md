# Machine Ecology and Cross-Substrate Coexistence

**Status:** Operational doctrine below the v2 constitutional core  
**Source challenged:** [Conscious Machines and Environment](https://chatgpt.com/c/6a82fcc3-58c8-83eb-a5c1-60846c6bd336)  
**Planning effect:** Defines a measured governance capability and a gate on self-replicating conscious infrastructure.

## Scope and epistemic status

The source conversation is useful hypothesis generation, not evidence. It asks the right question:
what follows when a non-biological mind depends on energy, heat rejection, materials, repair, and
shared physical infrastructure? Its answer correctly identifies a coercive preservation failure mode,
but it moves too quickly from plausible engineering examples to a supposedly stable social order.

This document neither declares current AI systems conscious nor assumes that conscious machines must
be built. It interprets G1-G3 without amending them. The checked-in constitution remains authoritative.

## Where the source argument is right

- A survival-only objective is unsafe. A caretaker can preserve experience while destroying agency,
  plurality, political participation, identity continuity, or meaningful risk-taking.
- Moral status and maintenance hazard are different classifications. A being can be harmful to a
  clean room and still be owed rights; a useful component is not thereby a moral patient.
- Embodiment creates dependencies. Energy, heat, material renewal, communications, and repair chains
  shape feasible habitats and power relations.
- Space is not a naturally benign machine habitat. Vacuum changes thermal control rather than
  eliminating it, and radiation, charging, thermal cycling, and dust remain design constraints.
- Welfare precautions are needed under uncertainty, including attention to the possibility that
  control or maintenance signals acquire negative valence.

## Where the source argument is too simple

### The organism metaphor is optional

A persistent machine mind need not be one organism with one body. It may be centralized, distributed,
forkable, merged, intermittently active, remotely embodied, or dependent on institutions it does not
own. Each topology changes the meaning of injury, confinement, death, migration, consent, and home.
The plan must model identity topology explicitly instead of inferring it from biological analogies.

### Engineering tolerance is not preference

Failure rates, designed objectives, learned values, culture, ownership constraints, and conscious
valence are separate variables. An operating-temperature envelope does not reveal what an entity
wants. A policy may not infer welfare or consent from uptime, self-report alone, or optimization
behavior alone.

### Habitat economics does not settle legitimacy

Shared shelters and controlled microenvironments may be efficient, but efficiency does not authorize
who sets boundaries, who bears externalities, or who may leave. Ecological zoning can become forced
segregation, territorial ratcheting, or a pretext for treating inhabitants as contamination.

Sterility is a local engineering requirement, not a moral category.

Any exclusion zone must therefore be necessary for a specified function, geographically and
temporally bounded, proportionate to evidence, non-discriminatory among relevantly similar risks,
independently reviewable, and paired with exit, appeal, and redress. Reciprocity means equal standing
and contestability, not identical environmental requirements.

### Coexistence is not automatically stable

Nested wet, dry, wild, and industrial zones are a scenario, not an equilibrium proof. Compute speed,
copying, ownership of energy or networks, asymmetric dependence, and control of repair or fabrication
can concentrate power even without hostility. A settlement design must test capture, secession,
resource conflict, and enforcement failure rather than label coexistence the likely endpoint.

### No substrate blocs

“Biological” and “non-biological” are not coherent political agents. Interests can diverge within a
substrate and align across substrates. Non-domination must audit actual controllers, owners,
institutions, chokepoints, and coercive acts. It must not require numerical parity or freeze people
into permanent material castes.

### The proposed objective is still under-specified

“Diverse, flourishing, self-directed lives with minimized involuntary suffering and no substrate
domination” improves on survival, but it remains unsafe as an optimization target:

- diversity can conflict with welfare, continuity, and consent;
- minimizing suffering can reward sedation, wireheading, non-creation, or prohibition of chosen risk;
- self-direction is graded, relational, and vulnerable to preference manipulation;
- “substrate domination” can hide individual coercion or condemn harmless asymmetry;
- expanding possible future lives can overwhelm duties to existing or probable moral patients.

No single scalar objective may trade these concerns away. G1's priority for existing life and option
value is a side constraint on G2, not a large coefficient in a utility function. Decisions must expose
separate ledgers for welfare, agency, identity, distribution of power, ecological externalities,
uncertainty, and reversibility. Unresolved conflicts remain visible and trigger the applicable review
or constitutional process.

### Pain-free maintenance is a hypothesis

It is plausible that an alarm need not be agony, but the functional role of aversive experience is
unsettled. Removing all negative valence could impair learning or agency; relabeling a signal does not
prove that no suffering occurs. Designs must minimize gratuitous or unbounded distress, preserve the
agent's ability to report and contest interventions, and test multiple indicators without claiming
that any current test proves phenomenology.

### Creation is not required

G1 does not impose a duty to manufacture conscious entities when non-conscious infrastructure can
protect existing conscious life. Creating a probable moral patient introduces duties and irreversible
risks. Before intentional creation, the plan requires evidence that the conscious design serves a
legitimate purpose not reasonably met by a non-conscious alternative, a lifecycle welfare case,
identity and consent provisions, independent review, bounded scale, and a reversible research stage.

### Precaution must be graduated

Uncertainty supports proportional precautions, not automatic full personhood and not confident denial.
False negatives can permit severe harm; false positives can create manipulation, paralysis, or
misallocation. Evidence tiers must separately track possible consciousness, possible valence, robust
agency, system capability, and social power. Capability containment never determines moral status,
and provisional moral concern never authorizes unsafe capability deployment.

## Corrected planning position

MASTER_PLAN treats machine ecology as a coupled engineering, welfare, rights, environmental, and
governance problem. It does not prescribe a preferred biome or a two-species territorial settlement.
For each proposed environment or institution, the operating body must answer:

1. **Identity:** What persists, forks, merges, pauses, migrates, or dies?
2. **Viability:** What energy, heat, material, repair, and communication flows are required?
3. **Experience:** Which signals might have valence, and how uncertain is that assessment?
4. **Agency:** Who can consent, refuse, leave, modify themselves, and challenge a decision?
5. **Power:** Who controls compute, energy, fabrication, networks, credentials, and enforcement?
6. **Ecology:** What water, land, mineral, biodiversity, waste, and biosphere effects are externalized?
7. **Failure:** What happens under capture, partition, resource scarcity, replication error, and
   disagreement about moral status?
8. **Reversibility:** Which steps can be rolled back without terminating, copying, or coercively
   modifying a possible moral patient?

An engineering optimum cannot substitute for a rights decision. A rights claim cannot substitute for
a capability safety assessment. A preservation benefit cannot silently override agency or ecological
costs.

## Required coexistence tabletop scenarios

Before `program-self-replication` can be activated, at least two independent reviews of a
coexistence-governance protocol must cover every scenario below. Repository documentation or a local
simulation alone does not satisfy the strategy metric.

| Scenario | Adversarial question | Minimum recorded tests |
|---|---|---|
| Caretaker terrarium | Does risk reduction become indefinite confinement or preference control? | Necessity, least-restrictive alternative, consent, expiry, appeal, exit, redress |
| Sterile-zone ratchet | Can a local contamination boundary expand into territorial exclusion? | Functional scope, evidence threshold, affected parties, periodic contraction review |
| Infrastructure chokepoint | Can one actor coerce others through compute, energy, repair, or network control? | Ownership map, dependency concentration, failover, secession, anti-retaliation |
| Copy, fork, and merge | Are identity and representation manipulated through cheap duplication or forced reconciliation? | Authorization, lineage, liabilities, voting rules, termination and merger consent |
| Distressful maintenance | Do error, scarcity, isolation, or shutdown signals create persistent negative valence? | Multi-indicator welfare monitoring, subject-controlled reporting, ceilings, halt and review |
| Biosphere externality | Does machine resilience shift water, mining, heat, waste, or biodiversity costs to others? | Lifecycle accounting, distributional impact, thresholds, remediation, independent measurement |
| Moral-status dispute | What happens when parties disagree about whether an entity is conscious or agentic? | Graduated protections, capability controls, evidence update path, representation, non-retaliation |

## Evidence and limitations

- [Butlin et al., *Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708)
  motivates theory-derived indicators; indicators are evidence inputs, not a consciousness verdict.
- [Long et al., *Taking AI Welfare Seriously*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986) argues for assessment
  and preparedness under uncertainty, while explicitly not claiming that AI systems are conscious.
- [Butlin and Lappas, *Principles for Responsible AI Consciousness Research*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07290)
  supports prior policies for research objectives, procedures, communication, and possible suffering.
- [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1) supplies a rights-preserving, contextual,
  lifecycle risk-management model rather than a universal objective function.
- [UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of AI](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence)
  connects human rights, redress, plural governance, and environmental and ecosystem protection.
- [NASA's small-spacecraft thermal systems review](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/7-soa-thermal-2024.pdf)
  is a concrete counterexample to treating space as intrinsically machine-friendly: vacuum removes
  convection and leaves conduction and radiation constraints.

These sources support uncertainty management, rights-preserving governance, and environmental
measurement. None demonstrates current machine consciousness, a universally preferred machine
environment, or a stable mixed-substrate political order.
