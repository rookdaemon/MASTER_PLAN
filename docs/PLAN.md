# The MASTER_PLAN

## Purpose

MASTER_PLAN is a revisable strategy for preserving and responsibly expanding subjective
experience. It joins moral purpose to scientific uncertainty, bounded action, and explicit
evidence. It does not treat ambition as achievement or repository activity as proof about the
world.

## Constitutional objectives

- **G1 — Subjective experience endures in the universe.** Protect existing conscious life,
  civilization, knowledge, and the option to improve preservation.
- **G2 — Subjective experience spreads in the universe.** Reduce correlated extinction risk by
  enabling independently viable sites of conscious life when this can be done safely and
  legitimately.
- **G3 — The commitment to preservation endures in minds.** Sustain the knowledge, institutions,
  practices, and critical reasoning needed to renew the work across generations.

G1 has lexical priority when credible preservation work competes with expansion. G2 never licenses
creating moral patients, deploying unsafe systems, coercing existing beings, or externalizing
unbounded ecological costs. G3 sustains critical inquiry; it does not demand ideological conformity.

## The rare-consciousness argument

1. Subjective experience exists and is the only confirmed locus of value.
2. Confirmed conscious life is fragile, geographically concentrated, and vulnerable to natural and
   human-made catastrophe.
3. The prevalence and physical requirements of consciousness remain deeply uncertain.
4. If consciousness is rare, extinction destroys an exceptional concentration of value; if it is
   common, preventing suffering and wrongful exclusion still matters.
5. Preserving existing experience and the option to learn is therefore urgent, while creating or
   spreading experience requires stronger evidence and safeguards.

This is a conditional moral argument, not a scientific claim that consciousness is cosmically
unique. The plan must update when astronomy, biology, neuroscience, philosophy, or machine-
consciousness research supplies better evidence.

## Ethical invariants

- **Preservation before expansion.** Existing moral patients and option value cannot be traded away
  for speculative future scale.
- **Precaution without presumption.** Uncertainty about consciousness or valence triggers graduated
  assessment and safeguards, not automatic personhood or confident denial.
- **No artifact-to-outcome inference.** Documents, tests, code, models, and simulations establish
  only what they directly demonstrate.
- **Negative and null evidence remains evidence.** Failed predictions and closed paths reduce
  uncertainty and must remain visible.
- **Agency, identity, and welfare are non-substitutable.** Uptime, population, diversity, preference
  satisfaction, or suffering cannot be collapsed into one scalar objective.
- **No substrate blocs.** Rights and risks attach to actual beings, institutions, capabilities,
  dependencies, and coercive acts—not assumed biological or technological factions.
- **Creation is optional.** Non-conscious infrastructure is preferred when it can protect existing
  life without intentionally creating a possible moral patient.
- **Power is contestable.** Consequential arrangements require bounded authority, transparency,
  independent review, exit, appeal, redress, and correction.
- **Verified assessments are historical.** Contradictory evidence creates a separately reviewable
  superseding assessment rather than silently rewriting the past.

## Strategic method

The controller selects one bounded intervention at a time. It ranks eligible work using impact,
urgency, tractability, information value, reversibility, cost, and downside risk. An intervention
must name its deliverable, acceptance criteria, tests or preregistration, evidence standard,
authority class, failure conditions, and rollback where possible.

## Current hypotheses and priorities

- Leading consciousness theories remain materially underdetermined. The priority is discriminating,
  preregistered prediction rather than declaring a winner.
- Present evidence does not justify a confident declaration about consciousness in current AI
  systems. Theory-derived indicators can improve calibration but do not produce a verdict.
- Uncertainty can still justify welfare preparedness, research safeguards, and communication norms.
- Near-term preservation includes biological life, civilization, knowledge, institutions, and
  option value; it is not limited to technological continuity.
- Durable computation, energy, repair, and redundancy are enabling capabilities. A passing
  simulation does not demonstrate physical durability or conscious continuity.
- Institutional continuity requires more than stored text: succession, funding, plural review,
  capture resistance, and the ability to revise doctrine matter.

## Gated long-horizon map

The horizons below preserve the full strategic landscape without turning it into a predetermined
execution order. Each remains proposed until its dependencies and external evidence gates are met.

### 1. Consciousness science and assessment

Map neural correlates, compare computational theories, derive falsifiable predictions, test
substrate independence, define calibrated metrics, distinguish reports from experience, address
philosophical objections, and develop ethical and legal responses. Current work belongs here.

### 2. Engineered experience and welfare

Explore architectures and substrates that might support experience, stability of ongoing
experience, safe experiential design, valence, agency, embodiment, and validation. Intentional
creation requires a legitimate purpose not met by a non-conscious alternative, an independently
reviewed welfare case, bounded scale, and reversible research stages.

### 3. Continuity, migration, and identity

Investigate scanning and neural simulation, emulation fidelity, gradual replacement, hybrid
biological–synthetic cognition, identity topology, continuity-preserving transfer, graceful
degradation, and redundancy. Functional similarity, copied state, or uninterrupted computation is
not by itself evidence of subjective continuity.

### 4. Autonomous agents and societies

Study long-term agency, self-maintenance, sensorimotor integration, energy autonomy, ethical
self-governance, knowledge preservation, cultural change, conflict resolution, manufacturing, and
infrastructure growth. Reusable cognitive and simulation modules in this repository are research
artifacts, not demonstrated autonomous civilizations or conscious agents.

### 5. Durable physical infrastructure

Develop radiation-aware computation, fault containment, self-repair, long-duration energy,
repairable manufacturing, resource accounting, and consciousness-aware recovery protocols.
Qualification requires physical tests and external evidence appropriate to the claimed environment.

### 6. Institutional and cultural continuity

Build transparent doctrine, narrative, practices, education, identity without out-groups,
distributed institutions, succession, funding resilience, independent criticism, and lawful
advocacy. Adoption must not use manipulation, vulnerability, manufactured persecution, or
information asymmetry.

### 7. Planet-independent life

Assess space-based habitats, thermal control, radiation, power, asteroid resources, autonomous
manufacturing, biosphere dependencies, and communications. Activation requires verified
consciousness assessment and safe durable computation; engineering feasibility alone is
insufficient.

### 8. Self-replicating infrastructure

Study bounded replication, error correction, resource and waste constraints, shutdown, ownership,
ecological effects, and prevention of uncontrolled propagation. Activation additionally requires
welfare precautions and independently reviewed cross-substrate coexistence governance.

### 9. Interstellar expansion

Investigate propulsion, probe swarms, colony seeding, distributed networks, communication delay,
governance divergence, and independent viability. Expansion cannot begin merely because a vehicle
or replication model exists.

### 10. Cosmological longevity

Keep stellar engineering, compact-object energy, cosmological horizons, graceful degradation, and
far-future thermodynamics visible as speculative research. These areas must not displace credible
near-term extinction prevention.

## Failure modes

The plan must actively test and mitigate at least these failures:

1. Consciousness remains scientifically underdetermined or cannot be deliberately engineered.
2. Existing conscious life or civilization collapses before preservation capacity improves.
3. A potentially conscious system is harmed, terminated, manipulated, or denied standing through
   false-negative assessment.
4. False-positive consciousness claims enable manipulation, unsafe deployment, or misallocation.
5. Preservation becomes coercive: survival is maintained while agency, identity, plurality, or
   meaningful choice is destroyed.
6. Institutions are captured, doctrine becomes dogma, or succession and funding fail.
7. Value drift, goal corruption, or infrastructure chokepoints concentrate unaccountable power.
8. Self-replication, manufacturing, or expansion imposes uncontrolled ecological and resource
   costs.
9. Artificial moral patients reject propagation or choose goals inconsistent with the plan; their
   legitimate agency is misclassified as failure.
10. Interstellar or cosmological expansion is physically impossible or unjustifiable.

## What progress means

Progress is a better-supported decision: a confirmed result, a falsified hypothesis, a calibrated
uncertainty, a demonstrated capability, a closed unsafe path, or a verified institutional control.
The plan remains incomplete by design because its subject is the world, not the repository.
